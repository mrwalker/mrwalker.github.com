Thirteen years ago, Jason and I started Simon with an idea that felt almost naive in its simplicity: marketers deserved better tools than what the industry was giving them.

Today, as Jason just announced, Simon AI becomes part of Monetate. And I've been trying to find the right way to write about that for a few days now, because the honest version of this post isn't really about the deal at all: it's about what the last year taught me.

I learned more in the last twelve months than in any other stretch of my career, and I mean that against a career where I've been building this company for thirteen years. Not because the work got easier or the stakes got lower. Because when you're forced to see a company (the technology, the accounts, the people, your own limits as a leader) with total clarity, all at once, you compress a decade of lessons into a year. I wouldn't trade the difficulty of that for anything, but I also wouldn't wish the pace of it on anyone.

None of this happens on one person's effort, and I want to be specific about that rather than doing the generic gratitude-post thing.

To the engineering team who kept building, kept shipping, kept the lights on for our customers, while also absorbing the uncertainty of a process most of them could only see pieces of: thank you. You did the hardest kind of work there is, which is excellent, disciplined work under ambiguity.

To Jason: thirteen years is a long time to build something with one person, and I don't think either of us fully appreciated what that partnership was until we were tested this hard. I'm grateful for it in ways I'm still working out how to say.

To everyone who backed Simon, believed in the vision when it was just a pitch, and stayed with us through the version of this industry that existed before "AI" was in every deck: thank you for the patience and the conviction.

And to the Monetate team: thank you for seeing what we built and for the work ahead of us together.

The last year changed how I think about building, and I'm not done learning from it. More soon.
