One of the biggest unlocks for our AI enablement workshop this week was the [Model Context Protocol](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/introduction) (MCP). Getting this setup wires the right tools and resources into an LLM, and watching it self-serve context is like magic.

MCP gave our Solutions Architecture team the scaffolding they needed to quickly start building Cyborg-style tools—AI assistants that work on their behalf and automate real, everyday tasks.

Because Cyborgs act as their user, they immediately benefit from MCP: they inherit permissions, pull personalized context, and start being useful without a heavy integration lift.

With MCP in place, the team didn't need to wait for centralized engineering or AI ops: they just started building!

MCP has its limitations—it's not built for high-throughput or tight security. So we needed our engineering and DevSecOps teams to pave the way building accessible knowledge bases and doing thorough security review of the tools before we opened them up to all Simon employees.

You'll find a rapidly growing list of public forks of open source MCP servers in our [Radico](https://github.com/Radico?q=mcp&type=public&language=&sort=) GitHub organization which have all gotten our internal stamp of approval. Thank you DevSecOps for all the hard work getting these going!
